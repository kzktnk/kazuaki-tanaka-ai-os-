## Purpose

Change management is **not** an announcement plan after go-live.

It is the work of:

1. **Readiness** — can this organization absorb this change now?  
2. **Strategy** — all at once, or in steps? in what cycles?  
3. **Commitment** — stakeholders move from unawareness to ownership while resistance is managed.

PMO **governs** transformation. This file **designs the people-side of the change**. Use together; do not collapse CM into status reporting.

See `frameworks/transformation-pmo.md` §Change Management Link and `standards/pmo-operating-guide.md` §Change Management Operating Standard.

---

## When to Use

- ERP / CIS / CRM / operating-model programs with large role change
- M&A, BPR, or “synergy” programs where finance is clear and adoption is not
- Before locking an all-at-once cutover
- When communications exist but understanding of “what it means for me” does not

## 1. Change readiness (before strategy)

Do not start with a communication calendar. First answer whether the organization can change.

### Workshop / assessment outcomes

A readiness pass should produce:

- Awareness of the **process** of change (not only the target design)
- Historical **critical success factors** from prior changes in *this* organization
- Anticipated **emotional responses** and likely behaviors
- A **change profile** (where the organization actually is vs target)
- Enablers vs **barriers**, prioritized
- An **action plan** against the top barriers

“If I were CEO for a day…” is a prompt to surface what leaders would actually stop, start, or protect — not a slogan.

### Readiness is not a survey score

Attitude is one slice. Alignment across at least:

| Dimension | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| Strategy / objectives | Is the change consistent with how the firm actually competes? |
| Structure and spans | Will the new work fit roles, layers, and decision rights? |
| Leadership and team dynamics | Will the leadership team sponsor, or only endorse? |
| Culture and assumptions | What unofficial rules will kill the official process? |
| Processes and technology | Do operations and systems support the new behavior? |
| History | What did the last change teach people to expect? |

Assessments worth distinguishing (methods, not product names):

- **Change history** — how previous programs were experienced  
- **Change readiness** — current capacity and barriers  
- **Change risk** — where the program is likely to stall  

Personality inventories and branded culture instruments stay outside this OS unless the client already runs them.

### Common error

Treating middle managers as a communication audience only. In improvement programs they are often the **primary resistance layer** — and the primary adoption layer.

## 2. Change strategy

### All-at-once vs one-step-at-a-time

| Strategy | Use when | Risk |
|----------|----------|------|
| **All-at-once** | Interlocking processes cannot run half-old / half-new; regulatory or cutover window is forced | Overwhelm, long silence then a spike of messages, no rehearsal of new behavior |
| **One-step-at-a-time** | Commitment and capability must be built; value can be released in slices | Drift, dual running forever, “temporary” workarounds becoming the model |

This is a **configuration choice**, not a moral preference. Sequence must match implementation milestones — and the employee’s lived calendar.

### Cycles

Strategy is not a single workshop. Source materials treat change as **repeating cycles of a small number of steps** (plan → act → learn → adjust). Do not invent a four-box brand; keep the idea: **iterate**, do not announce once.

## 3. Build commitment

Workstreams that belong in the CM plan (names generalized from LPD CM):

| Workstream | Job |
|------------|-----|
| **Stakeholder management** | Who must act, who is impacted, who is critical to success; **action log**; lead contacts |
| **Resistance** | Expected, designed for — not a surprise at go-live |
| **Communications** | Time-phased messages; schedule on a page |
| **Team-building** | The team that must operate the new model, not only the project team |
| **Transfer** | Knowledge and authority actually move to the line |
| **Training / enablement** | Strategy → materials → delivery → evaluate — a workstream, not a day of product training |

### Stakeholder progression

Already in the PMO standard; repeated here because CM owns the **interventions**:

```
Unawareness → Awareness → Buy-in → Ownership
```

Lead contacts exist to **influence and pass issues both ways**. They are not gatekeepers who filter bad news.

### Three understandings every person needs

Communications fail when only the corporate “why” is told.

1. Why the organization is changing  
2. What it means for **this unit**  
3. What it means for **me**

Test: can a front-line supervisor answer all three without a slide?

### Time-phased messages

- Map messages to **milestones**, not to a weekly newsletter habit  
- Avoid long **gaps** (people fill them with rumor) and **spikes** (too many asks in one week)  
- Employee test: sit in their calendar and see if the sequence is livable  
- Journalist prompts for each message: What / Why / When / Who / Where / How  
- Specify **who speaks** and who prepares behind the scenes  

After go-live, the tone shifts: we heard you; the design is not frozen; here is how to get help (**soft landing**), then **performance maximization** — not “the project is over.”

Quick wins belong in the emotional curve (confusion → discouragement → **quick wins** → proficiency). They are designed, not accidental.
