# No.14 — Who Owns AI Decisions?

## Original English Article

Who Owns AI Decisions?

We often ask whether AI can make decisions.

But perhaps the more important question is:

> Who owns those decisions?

As AI evolves from generating content to recommending actions and executing workflows, organizations are increasingly delegating decisions to AI systems.

An AI agent may prioritize maintenance activities.
It may recommend shutting down equipment.
It may allocate resources during an operational incident.

Technically, AI can support—or even execute—these decisions.

But delegation is not the same as ownership.

Decision ownership begins where technical execution ends.

It means accepting responsibility for the consequences.

This distinction becomes especially important in critical industries such as energy, finance, healthcare, public services, and defense.

The key question is no longer only:

> Can AI make the right decision?

It is also:

> Who accepts the outcome when the decision proves wrong?

An algorithm cannot carry legal liability.
It cannot hold safety responsibility.
It cannot explain itself to regulators, customers, employees, or society.

Those obligations remain with designated individuals and institutions.

Core concepts:

- Decision Execution
- Decision Ownership

AI may execute decisions.

Organizations still need someone who owns them.
