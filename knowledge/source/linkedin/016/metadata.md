## Core Theme

An AI agent is not a risk owner. Organizations delegate work to AI—not accountability. Operational AI scales execution while humans and institutions retain risk ownership.

## Key Distinctions

| Common framing | Reframed question |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Can AI make this decision? | Who continues to own the risk after AI makes the recommendation? |
| AI as "agent" | Agent ≠ risk owner |
| Replace human responsibility | Humans own risk; AI scales execution |

## Risk Owner Expectations

- understand consequences
- balance competing objectives
- accept accountability
- justify decisions to regulators, boards, society

## Non-delegable to AI (today)

- legal responsibility
- regulatory appearance
- board-level explanation
- societal accountability

## Execution vs Ownership Split

| Dimension | May shift to AI | Remains human / institutional |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------------------|
| Decision execution | Increasingly | — |
| Risk ownership | — | Fundamentally |

## Relationship to No.14

No.14: Decision Execution vs Decision Ownership (consequences, override, explain).

No.16: reframes ownership around **risk**—who holds risk after AI recommends or acts. Complementary; risk ownership is the operational stake that decision ownership must preserve.

## Relationship to No.17

No.16 establishes that risk cannot be transferred to AI.

No.17 adds: even when capable, AI authority must be designed per decision—authority grants do not transfer risk ownership.

Combined:

> Work and execution may delegate to AI. Risk ownership and accountability remain assigned to humans and institutions.
