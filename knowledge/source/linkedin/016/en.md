# No.16 — Can AI Own Risk?

## Source type

LinkedIn English version

## Original article

AI agents are becoming increasingly capable.

They can analyze situations, recommend actions, and even execute complex workflows.

But here's a question I don't hear often enough:

Can AI actually own risk?

Organizations often describe AI as an "agent."

Yet an agent is not automatically a risk owner.

A risk owner is expected to understand consequences, balance competing objectives, accept accountability, and justify decisions to others.

Today's AI can assist with many of these tasks.

But it cannot assume legal responsibility.

It cannot appear before a regulator.

It cannot explain its judgment to a board of directors.

And it cannot be held accountable by society.

That's why organizations shouldn't ask,

"Can AI make this decision?"

Instead, they should ask,

"Who continues to own the risk after AI makes the recommendation?"

This distinction is especially important in sectors such as energy, healthcare, finance, defense, and critical infrastructure.

As AI autonomy increases, organizations will increasingly separate two different concepts:

* Decision execution
* Risk ownership

The first may become increasingly autonomous.

The second will remain fundamentally human.

Perhaps the future of Operational AI isn't about replacing human responsibility.

It's about enabling humans to own risk while allowing AI to scale execution.

Because organizations ultimately don't delegate accountability.

They delegate work.
