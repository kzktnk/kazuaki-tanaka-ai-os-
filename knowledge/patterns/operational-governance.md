# Pattern: Operational Governance

**Status:** Active  
**Origin:** LinkedIn No.7, No.10, SP09

## Pattern statement

AI governance must become an operational capability—not merely a set of rules documented outside day-to-day execution.

## Core distinction

### Policy Governance

Rules, standards, approval flows, and compliance documentation reviewed periodically.

### Operational Governance

The ability to continuously align AI decisions, human accountability, business objectives, and operational reality within daily execution.

## Signals

Use this pattern when:

- AI governance is treated as a static policy manual
- AI agents are entering core operations (maintenance, incidents, customer response)
- organizations cannot detect failures, escalate exceptions, or maintain auditability at operational speed
- governance committees exist but operational teams lack embedded controls

## Operational capabilities (not just technical features)

- logging and traceability
- model monitoring
- authority and permission control
- escalation paths
- Human-in-the-Loop design
- exception handling
- accountability assignment
- adaptation to changing conditions

## Design response

Operational Governance requires connecting:

- **Strategy** — business objectives and risk appetite
- **Operations** — how AI is run daily
- **Technology** — controls that make governance executable

Governance must be embedded where AI acts—not only where policies are written.

## Core rule

> The future of governance is not bureaucracy. It is operational capability.
